# How should I live if nothing guarantees that any of it matters?

*Status: open · last touched 2026-07-24 · sources checked 2026-07-24*

## The question

There is a sentence at the front of *The Myth of Sisyphus* that refuses to stay in its
book: "There is but one truly serious philosophical problem, and that is suicide."
[VERIFY: exact O'Brien wording] Whatever else Camus meant, he meant something about
priority. Before metaphysics, before epistemology, there is a question that does not
wait — is this worth continuing? — and it is being answered every day, by everyone,
mostly without words.

The question of this note, in its plain form: **how should I live if nothing guarantees
that any of it matters?** Not "does life have a meaning" as a piece of cosmology, but
what follows for an actual Monday morning from the possibility that the universe
underwrites none of it.

But there are two questions here, and half the work of this note is keeping them apart:

1. **The diagnostic question.** What exactly would a "guarantee of meaning" *be*? What
   is the thing whose absence is being mourned — and is the demand for it so much as
   coherent?
2. **The practical question.** Given the answer to the first, how should I live? What
   survives of purpose, engagement, and care once the guarantee is off the table?

Most arguments about the meaning of life slide between these two without noticing —
metaphysical premises smuggled into practical advice, practical comfort offered as if it
settled the metaphysics. I will try to keep the seam visible.

The rest of this repository owes this note a debt it has been deferring. The whole
`continuity/` folder — [[immortality-desirability]] most of all — argues about whether
more life would be worth wanting, and quietly assumes that *worth wanting* is a notion
in good standing. This note is where that assumption comes to be examined.

And what would a real answer even look like? Not a taxonomy of positions. A posture I
could actually hold — and hold at both times of day: at noon, when the question feels
academic, and at three in the morning, when it does not. That is the test I will keep
returning to below: an answer that convinces at only one of those hours is probably not
an answer.
